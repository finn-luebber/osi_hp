Open access means that research results can be read by anyone, free of charge and without technical barriers. Readers do not need a subscription, and authors usually keep the right to share and reuse their own work. Open the sections below to read more.

## Why open access?

Research paid for by the public should be available to the public – to patients, practitioners, teachers, journalists and researchers at institutions that cannot afford every subscription.

Open access also helps research itself: openly available articles are easier to find, read, check and build on. Many funders, including the DFG and the EU's Horizon Europe programme, now expect the results of the projects they fund to be published open access.

## How to do open access?

There are several routes, and they can be combined:

- **Gold:** the article is free to read in the journal itself, often paid for by a publication fee.
- **Green:** the authors place a version of the article in a repository, sometimes after an embargo.
- **Diamond:** the journal is free for readers and for authors.

Before you submit, check the journal's open access policy and your funder's requirements. Preprints, for example on [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org) or [PsyArXiv](https://psyarxiv.com), make results available even before peer review.
